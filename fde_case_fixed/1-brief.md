# FDE case study, Spyne

Time: about two hours. Please do not spend more than three.

## The seat

You will run US dealership service accounts on a night shift. When a dealer's
phone agent mishandles a call at 2 am, you are the person who reads that call,
finds why, and either fixes it or writes it up for a dev.

This case is the same job on a smaller system. The workshop is in Pune so you do
not have to learn US auto retail first.

## Context

Sparsh Motors runs four car service workshops in Pune. Multi brand, mostly
regular servicing, repairs and insurance jobs.

Customers call to book a slot, ask what a job will cost, or check whether their
car is ready. Service advisors are on the floor most of the day, so a lot of
calls go unanswered.

Six weeks ago Sparsh started using an AI phone assistant. After four unanswered
rings the assistant takes the call.

The pack holds one week from one workshop. Twenty calls, each with its
transcript, the outcome the assistant logged, and its tool log. Plus the weekly
summary Sparsh management was sent, and a one page reference for the tools.

## What we want

Read the pack. Work out what is going wrong. Then write the seven things below.

We are not telling you what the problem is. Working that out is the case study.

## Deliverables, in this order

a. What actually happened this week. Under 250 words. Every number carries its
   denominator. 4 of 20 is a number, 20% on its own is not.

b. One call traced end to end through its tool log. Name the tool call where it
   went wrong and say why.

c. A note to the workshop manager. Under 80 words, plain language, and nothing
   promised that you cannot keep.

d. A ticket for engineering. Ten lines. A dev who has never seen this pack has
   to be able to act on it cold.

e. One fix you would make yourself tonight. A prompt change, a config change, a
   small script. Say how you would check tomorrow that it worked.

f. Two places the AI gave you an answer and you did not take it. What it said,
   and what you did instead.

g. Your session log or prompt history.

## On using AI

Use it heavily. Claude Code, Codex, whatever you work with. Send the session log
with your answer.

The pack is too big to eyeball in two hours. That is deliberate. We are testing
what you ask it, what you verify yourself, and what you throw away.

A clean answer with no working history behind it counts against you.

## Limits

800 words total across a to f. The limit is firm. Exhibits and the session log
do not count towards it.

Any number you use needs a source inside the pack. Point at the call ID or the
tool log line. If you do not have a source, say you do not have one.

If something in the pack is unclear, make an assumption and write it down rather
than asking us.

The submission deadline is in the email you received with this pack.

## After you send it

Fifteen minutes live with two of us. We will stop anywhere in your trace and ask
what it does and why. Come ready to defend the choices you took from the AI as
well as your own.

Then we send you three lines of feedback. You send a v2 within 24 hours. How
much the v2 moves is part of the score.

You get feedback either way, whether we move forward or not.
